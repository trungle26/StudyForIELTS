# Learning Method

This document translates the learning approach described in the supplied Ngoc Bach self-study guide into product principles. It is a design reference, not a reproduction of the book or its copyrighted lesson content.

## Core sequence

```text
Assess level
  -> Build basic grammar and pronunciation
  -> Learn topic vocabulary
  -> Improve general reading and listening
  -> Practise the four IELTS skills
  -> Learn question types and exam strategies
  -> Submit work for feedback
  -> Review errors and repeat weak areas
```

## Stage 1: Foundation English

For learners from beginner level through approximately IELTS 4.5–5.5:

- Basic grammar before complex essay structures.
- Pronunciation, phonemic awareness, word stress, and dictionary use.
- Topic-based vocabulary learned in context.
- Short reading texts with comprehension checks.
- Listening with transcripts, replay, and dictation.
- Short controlled writing before full IELTS essays.
- Basic speaking practice through repetition and recorded responses.

The app should not make advanced IELTS strategy the first task for an A1 or A2 learner.

## Stage 2: IELTS preparation

For learners who have enough foundation to benefit from exam preparation:

- IELTS listening sections and question types.
- IELTS reading passages and question types.
- Speaking Part 1, Part 2, and Part 3 practice.
- Task 1 chart/report writing.
- Task 2 essay planning and writing.
- Model-answer analysis, timed practice, and error review.
- Band-targeted strategies after core language ability is established.

## Learning loop

Every lesson should follow the smallest useful loop:

```text
Learn
  -> Try
  -> Receive a clear result
  -> Review the error
  -> Retry or schedule review
```

For foundation learners, results should focus on observable skills rather than an IELTS band. Examples:

- Correct sentence structure.
- Correct use of an article.
- Number of words recognized in a recording.
- Vocabulary recalled after a delay.
- Ability to identify the main idea.

## Content progression

Content should move from:

- Controlled to open-ended.
- Short to long.
- Slow and supported to natural speed.
- Familiar topics to abstract topics.
- General English to IELTS-specific tasks.
- Immediate correction to independent self-review.

## Vocabulary method

Vocabulary should be organized by topic and reinforced through:

1. Meaning in context.
2. Pronunciation and phonetic support.
3. Example sentences.
4. Word families and collocations when appropriate.
5. Retrieval practice.
6. Review of previously missed items.

Do not rely on isolated word lists as the primary learning experience.

## Listening method

The existing dictation feature is a good foundation. Extend it progressively:

1. Listen for the general meaning.
2. Listen with transcript support.
3. Replay short segments.
4. Type or complete missing words.
5. Compare with the transcript.
6. Review vocabulary and pronunciation.
7. Repeat at a more natural speed.

## Feedback rules

- Feedback must match the learner's stage.
- Do not assign an IELTS band to a beginner grammar or sentence exercise.
- Show one or two actionable corrections before giving a long explanation.
- Preserve the learner's answer and explain why a correction is needed.
- Allow retry after feedback.
- Avoid making color the only signal of correctness.

## Content authoring rules

- Use original examples or content with clear licensing.
- Tag every lesson with level, skill, topic, and learning stage.
- Keep lessons small enough to complete in a short session.
- Include prerequisites when a concept depends on earlier knowledge.
- Include a measurable completion condition.
- Prefer stable structured content over prompts that generate unpredictable lessons.

## Source relationship

The supplied guide informs the order and teaching philosophy:

- Foundation first.
- Topic vocabulary.
- Pronunciation and transcript-supported listening.
- Reading development.
- Four-skill IELTS preparation.
- Practical self-study and review.

The app must create its own implementation, examples, and content contracts rather than copying the source text.
